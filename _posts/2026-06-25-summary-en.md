## [Generative AI for homework may lower Chinese students' exam scores](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21577) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A 30-month study of 26,811 Chinese students in grades 7–12 found that using generative AI for homework improved homework scores by 18% and reduced completion time by 30%, but led to a 20% drop in closed-book monthly exam scores within 6 months and an 18%–24% decline in high-stakes exam scores like the Zhongkao and Gaokao. This large-scale longitudinal study provides strong evidence that generative AI can undermine deep learning and long-term academic performance, especially for students who outsource homework to AI, which has critical implications for educators and policymakers integrating AI into classrooms. The negative effects varied by subject, with social sciences suffering the largest losses, followed by STEM and languages; lower-grade, high-achieving, and male students were more affected. About 80% of AI users exhibited an 'homework outsourcing' pattern—very short homework time but high scores—and bore most of the performance decline.

**Background**: Generative AI, such as large language models, can produce human-like text and solve problems, making it attractive for students to use for homework. However, when students rely on AI to complete assignments without engaging with the material, they may fail to develop the knowledge and skills needed for closed-book exams. This study tracked students over 30 months, providing a comprehensive view of AI's impact on learning.

## [14 Sloppy Shuffles Needed to Randomize a Deck](https://www.quantamagazine.org/seven-perfect-shuffles-randomize-a-deck-of-cards-but-how-many-sloppy-ones-20260617/) ⭐️ 7.0/10
