## [Huawei Proposes 'Tao's Law' for Semiconductor Scaling](https://www.peopleapp.com/column/30052220655-500007509895) ⭐️ 8.0/10

At the 2026 IEEE International Symposium on Circuits and Systems in Shanghai, Huawei unveiled 'Tao's Law', a new semiconductor scaling principle that replaces traditional geometric scaling with 'time scaling'. Over the past six years, Huawei has designed and mass-produced 381 chips based on this principle, and a new Kirin mobile chip using logic folding technology is expected in fall 2026. Tao's Law offers a potential path to continue semiconductor performance improvements beyond the physical limits of Moore's Law, which is increasingly constrained by quantum tunneling and soaring costs. If validated, this could reshape the global chip industry and reduce reliance on extreme ultraviolet lithography, with significant implications for Huawei and China's semiconductor self-sufficiency. Tao's Law targets systematic reduction of the time constant τ across device, circuit, chip, and system levels, using techniques like logic folding. Huawei projects that by 2031, high-end chips based on this law could achieve transistor density equivalent to 1.4nm process technology.

telegram · zaihuapd · May 25, 01:35

**Background**: Moore's Law, which predicted that transistor density would double every two years, has driven semiconductor advancement for decades but is now slowing due to physical and economic barriers. Traditional geometric scaling shrinks transistor dimensions, but at nanometer scales, issues like quantum tunneling and escalating fabrication costs emerge. Tao's Law proposes a complementary approach: instead of shrinking physical sizes, it reduces signal propagation delays (time constants) to improve performance and density, potentially extending the life of silicon-based chips without requiring extreme lithography.

Mullvad has announced a mitigation rollout against exit IP fingerprinting, a technique that could correlate VPN users across different websites by exploiting deterministic IP assignment patterns. This update addresses a significant privacy vulnerability that could undermine the anonymity of VPN users, especially those relying on Mullvad for sensitive activities. It sets a precedent for other VPN providers to follow in combating advanced fingerprinting techniques. The mitigation modifies how exit IPs are assigned to users, breaking the deterministic pattern that allowed cross-site correlation. Mullvad's multihop feature, which routes traffic through two servers, further complicates fingerprinting attempts.

hackernews · Cider9986 · May 25, 17:45 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48269580)

**Background**: Exit IP fingerprinting is a technique where websites observe the IP address assigned to a VPN user and, because some VPNs assign IPs deterministically based on the user's WireGuard key, can link the same user across different sites even if they switch servers. Mullvad previously distributed users across multiple exit IPs per server to reduce congestion, but this created recognizable patterns. Browser fingerprinting, such as canvas fingerprinting, is a related technique that identifies users based on device characteristics.

## [EU Preliminary: Google May Have Violated DMA](https://t.me/zaihuapd/41566) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The European Commission has issued a preliminary finding that Alphabet (Google) may have violated the Digital Markets Act (DMA) by self-preferencing its own services in search results and restricting Play Store developers from steering users to alternative purchasing channels. This is the first major enforcement action under the DMA against a gatekeeper, potentially setting a precedent for how self-preferencing and anti-steering rules are applied, and could force Google to significantly alter its search and app store practices across the EU. The investigation focuses on Google Search allegedly favoring its own shopping, flight, and hotel services, and on Play Store rules that prevent developers from informing users about cheaper alternatives outside the app. Despite Google's previous compliance measures, the Commission considers them insufficient.

**Background**: The Digital Markets Act (DMA) is an EU regulation that designates large online platforms as 'gatekeepers' and imposes obligations to ensure fair competition. One key obligation is to refrain from self-preferencing, i.e., ranking a gatekeeper's own products or services more favorably than those of third parties. Another is to allow app developers to steer users to offers outside the gatekeeper's platform. Google was designated a gatekeeper for its search engine and app store in 2023.
