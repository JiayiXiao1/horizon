## [Chemistry Behind Garden Grove MMA Tank Incident](https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/methyl-methacrylate-tank) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A detailed chemical analysis of the Garden Grove methyl methacrylate (MMA) tank incident reveals a runaway polymerization reaction, likely triggered by heat or contamination, leading to a hazardous exothermic event. This incident highlights the critical need for robust safety measures in industrial chemical storage, as runaway polymerization can cause catastrophic failures like BLEVE (Boiling Liquid Expanding Vapor Explosion). Methyl methacrylate polymerization exhibits a strong gel effect (autoacceleration) around 20% conversion, where reaction rate and heat generation increase rapidly, making thermal runaway a significant hazard.

hackernews · nooks · May 26, 19:25 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48284712)

**Background**: Methyl methacrylate (MMA) is a monomer used to produce polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) plastics. Its free-radical polymerization is exothermic and can undergo autoacceleration, where viscosity increases trap radicals, accelerating the reaction. Without proper cooling or inhibition, this can lead to thermal runaway and potential explosion.
