## [Running GLM 5.2 on a 32GB Laptop with Colibrì](https://github.com/JustVugg/colibri) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A developer created Colibrì, a single-file C engine that runs the 744B-parameter GLM 5.2 Mixture-of-Experts model on a 12-core laptop with 32GB RAM using int4 quantization and on-demand expert streaming from disk. This demonstrates that state-of-the-art LLMs with hundreds of billions of parameters can be made accessible on consumer hardware, potentially democratizing powerful AI for individuals without expensive GPUs. The model activates only ~40B parameters per token, with ~17B dense parameters resident in RAM at int4 (~9.9 GB) and 21,504 routed experts stored on disk (~370 GB) streamed on demand with an LRU cache. The engine is a single ~1,300-line C file with no BLAS, Python, or GPU dependencies.

hackernews · vforno · Jul 9, 08:05 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48842459)

**Background**: GLM 5.2 is a 744B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLM with 1M-token context, designed for long-horizon tasks. Int4 quantization reduces model weights to 4-bit integers, cutting memory usage significantly. Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) is a technique that predicts multiple tokens at once to speed up generation.

## [No Leap Second at End of December 2026](https://datacenter.iers.org/data/latestVersion/bulletinC.txt) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS) announced that no leap second will be introduced at the end of December 2026, keeping the UTC-TAI offset at -37 seconds. This decision affects global timekeeping and digital systems that rely on precise time synchronization, as leap seconds can cause disruptions in computer networks, financial transactions, and satellite operations. The last leap second was added on December 31, 2016, and since then UTC has been exactly 37 seconds behind TAI. The IERS typically announces leap seconds about six months in advance, and this bulletin confirms no adjustment is needed for the end of 2026.

hackernews · ChrisArchitect · Jul 9, 14:16 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48846281)

**Background**: A leap second is a one-second adjustment occasionally applied to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) to keep it synchronized with astronomical time (UT1), which varies due to irregularities in Earth's rotation. Since 1972, 27 positive leap seconds have been added, all at the end of June or December. The decision is made by the IERS to ensure the difference between UTC and UT1 does not exceed ±0.9 seconds.
