## [Exchanges Shut LAN Lines, Nearby Data Center Rents Surge](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/lH2IAcm1uX33Hw1H_EfPDg) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On the evening of July 31, 2026, Chinese stock exchanges (Shanghai, Shenzhen, Beijing) shut down LAN-based trading and market data lines within their data centers, mandating a switch to WAN connections with a two-way latency limit of at least 2 milliseconds. This has triggered a surge in rents for nearby financial data centers, with standard 4000W racks rising from about 7,000 yuan to around 10,000 yuan per month, and some prime locations doubling. This regulatory change eliminates the physical proximity advantage that high-frequency trading (HFT) firms previously enjoyed, leveling the playing field but also sparking a scramble for nearby data center space. It affects HFT strategies, data center pricing, and the broader market structure, potentially reducing ultra-short-term speculative trading. The policy requires all institutions to connect via WAN with a minimum two-way latency of 2ms, effectively removing the speed edge of co-location. Only a few thousand financial-grade third-party racks exist near the exchanges in areas like Jinqiao, Waigaoqiao, and Zhangjiang, leading to supply shortages and rent spikes.
