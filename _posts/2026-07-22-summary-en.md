## [Ben Thompson Proposes US Law to Legalize AI Training Data as Fair Use](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/20/afraid-of-chinese-models/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Ben Thompson proposed that the US should pass a law explicitly making data collection for AI training fair use and barring terms of service that forbid distillation, to help US open models compete with Chinese counterparts. He also noted that Alibaba's release of Qwen 3.8 Max as open weights may have been influenced by a recent speech by Xi Jinping encouraging open source. This proposal addresses the hypocrisy of AI labs banning distillation on their models while training on unlicensed data, and could reshape US AI policy to foster innovation and competition. If enacted, it would provide legal clarity for training data and enable broader access to model capabilities through distillation, potentially leveling the playing field with Chinese AI models. The proposal includes two key provisions: (1) making training data collection explicit fair use, and (2) barring terms of service that forbid distillation for US companies. Thompson argues that stopping distillation is nearly impossible since it's just querying an API, so the US should instead lean into a policy that indemnifies labs and guarantees further innovation.

**Background**: AI model distillation is a technique where a smaller 'student' model learns from a larger 'teacher' model, often by querying its API, to achieve similar performance with less compute. The legal status of training AI on copyrighted data is currently disputed in US courts, with fair use being a key defense. Open-weight models, like Qwen 3.8 Max, release their trained parameters publicly, allowing anyone to download and run them.
